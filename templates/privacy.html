This site is run by one person, so there is no data protection officer.

What is stored, and why

  • Your account: username, password (only as a salted hash), the papers you save, mark as "not for this tag" or neutral, per tag, which papers you marked as read and when, your latest digest and how many papers per tag it should have, and when you were last active. So that recommendations load fast, the model trained on a tag's papers (numbers computed from them, nothing else) is kept too; it is replaced when the tag changes and erased with the tag. Needed to provide the service you signed up for (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). Kept until you delete the account.
  • Your email address, only if you enter one: used for nothing but the weekly digest, which only starts after you confirm the address through a link we mail to it (double opt-in). We store when you confirmed, as the record of your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR). You can withdraw any time through the link in every digest or on your profile; the address is then deleted.
  • Papers you add by link: if a paper isn't here yet, this server asks arXiv for it. The request contains only the paper's id, nothing about you. We keep a log of who added which paper for two days, to enforce daily limits.
  • References and thumbnails: to show which papers your saved papers cite, and a picture of each paper, the server downloads the public HTML version and PDF of saved and recommended papers from arXiv and keeps their reference lists, and, for papers under a Creative Commons license, a small image of their first figure and first page, credited with that license. The requests contain only the paper's id, nothing about you, and none of this says who saved a paper.
  • Technical data: to keep the site working and protect it from abuse (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR), your IP address is held in memory for rate limits and forgotten within minutes, and an address entered for the digest is held in memory for a day, to limit confirmation mails. The application logs record requests without IP addresses or search terms; the mail server logs record recipient addresses of delivered mail. All logs are rotated (at most 30 MB per service). For the statistics page, the server counts how long each kind of page takes to load, per minute, without any user, IP address or search term, and keeps those counts for a week. The reverse proxy in front of the site may log IP addresses for a short time as part of its standard operation.
  • Public profile: while your profile is public, anyone with a link to your pages (they contain your username) can view your tags, saved papers, recommendations and digest, and copy a tag into their own account; never your email address. Right after signing up you are asked whether your profile should be public (ticked by default; until you answer it is private), and you can make it private any time on your profile, which ends that access at once.

Cookies and third parties

The only cookie is the session cookie that keeps you logged in, which is strictly necessary for the service (§ 25(2) TDDDG, Art. 5(3) ePrivacy Directive), so no consent is asked for. There is no tracking, no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party content: scripts and fonts come from this server, so your browser contacts no one else. Links to arXiv lead to arXiv's own site. The one other thing kept in your browser is whether you picked the "Cards" or "Titles only" view (local storage, never sent to the server), a setting you asked for (§ 25(2) TDDDG).

Who receives your data

No one, apart from the hosting provider{{ ' (' + operator.hosting + ')' if operator.hosting }}, which runs the server under a data processing agreement (Art. 28 GDPR). Digests are delivered directly by this server to your mail provider. Nothing is sold or shared.

Backups

One backup copy of the account database is made daily and overwrites the previous one, so data you delete is gone from it within a day.

Your rights

You can access and download everything stored about you (download my data, Art. 15 and 20 GDPR), correct it (Art. 16), delete your account yourself on your profile (Art. 17), and restrict or object to processing (Art. 18 and 21). Write to the address above for anything else. You can also complain to a data protection supervisory authority, for example the one where you live.
